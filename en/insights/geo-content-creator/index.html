AI is replacing search engines: why content creators now need to optimize for GEO

Search is being turned upside down: AI models like Gemini, ChatGPT and Perplexity give direct answers instead of lists of links. How AIs pick their sources and what that means for you.

  • AI
  • SEO & GEO

Online search is facing a massive upheaval. For years Google was the undisputed top dog, with algorithms that ranked websites by relevance and authority. But with the rapid rise of artificial intelligence (AI), a new era is taking shape: search engines are increasingly turning into answer engines, powered by AI models like Google’s Gemini, OpenAI’s ChatGPT or Perplexity. What does that mean for you as a content creator, or for your business? How do you make sure your content gets found in this new landscape – especially when it comes to local searches (GEO criteria)?

From link to answer: the new search landscape

Until now, the goal of search engine optimization (SEO) was to show up on the first page of results. That meant using good keywords, building high-quality backlinks and taking care of technical SEO. AI-driven platforms take a different route. They try to deliver the answer to a user’s question directly, instead of just a list of links. And this is the important part: Google’s AI Overview is currently rolling out in the US and is coming to Germany soon. That means users see an AI-generated summary right at the top of the search results, often without ever having to click through to a website.

How do AIs choose their content?

That’s the core question keeping all of us busy. The exact algorithms behind the AI models are trade secrets, but we can make a few well-founded assumptions:

  • Relevance and authority (reinterpreted): AIs no longer judge content just by keywords, but by its ability to deliver a precise and complete answer. A source’s authority will still matter, but so will the clarity and structure of the content. A well-structured FAQ section or a concise paragraph can be worth more to an AI than pages of text with no clear organization.
  • Accuracy and objectivity: AIs are trained to process facts. Content that is well researched, referenced and objective gets preferred. Speculation or pure opinion could lose weight, unless it’s clearly labeled as such and embedded in a context of relevant information.
  • Natural language processing (NLP): The AI’s ability to understand and generate natural language means content written in natural, human-sounding language can be processed better. Keyword stuffing or unnatural phrasing is more likely to put the AI off.
  • Depth and breadth of data: AIs probably prefer sources that cover a topic thoroughly and shed light on different perspectives. A short blog post that only scratches the surface will carry less weight than a detailed resource.
  • User engagement (indirect): Even if AIs don’t directly measure click behavior on your website, they might use indirect signals from behavior on other platforms. If your content is shared or discussed a lot on social media, that could be a sign of its relevance and quality.
  • “Hidden” signals: AIs are likely to pay attention to less obvious signals too, like how up to date the information is, page load time (in case they do need to link to the source) and mobile friendliness.

Gemini, ChatGPT, Perplexity & co. – who calls the tune?

  • Gemini (Google): As Google’s answer to the AI boom, Gemini is especially relevant because it’s being built straight into Google Search (think “AI Overviews”). Here it’s crucial that content is prepared so Gemini can easily interpret it and turn it into concise answers. The emphasis is on answering questions directly and summarizing information.
  • ChatGPT (OpenAI): ChatGPT is known for its ability to generate human-like text and answer complex questions. Content optimized for ChatGPT should be clearly structured, have concise headings and be written in fluent, natural language.
  • Perplexity AI: Perplexity differs from the others in that it always cites its sources. For you as a content creator, that’s a big opportunity: if your website is identified and linked as a trustworthy source, that’s a huge advantage. Here it’s all about delivering authoritative, well-supported information.

It’s important to understand that these platforms learn from each other and influence each other. Optimizing for one AI platform will probably benefit the others too.

GEO optimization in the AI era: the be-all and end-all for local businesses

For local businesses and service providers, GEO optimization (local SEO) matters more than ever. If AIs deliver direct answers, you need to make sure those answers include your local information. AI Overviews will also take local searches into account, so your visibility here is crucial.

What you can do as a content creator / website copywriter to optimize your site for GEO criteria:

  1. Create detailed local content: Go beyond just stating your address. Create specific landing pages for each of your locations or service areas. Describe in detail which services you offer there, what makes them special, or which local events you support.\
    • Example: Instead of “Bakery in Munich”, write “Our organic bakery in Munich-Schwabing: handcrafted bread from the region and fresh Brezn (Bavarian pretzels) every day.”
  2. Anticipate “near me” searches: People increasingly search for “services near me”. Work this kind of phrasing into your copy, but naturally, not forced.
    • Example: “Find the best hairdresser near you in Berlin-Mitte” or “Italian food delivery near the main station in Cologne.”
  3. Optimize Google My Business (GMB) / Google Business Profile: This is and remains the absolute foundation for local searches.
    • Make sure your information is complete and current (name, address, phone number, opening hours, website).
    • Add high-quality photos.
    • Collect and respond to reviews. Positive reviews are a strong signal of trust and quality that AIs can pick up on too.
    • Use the posts feature for current offers or news.
  4. NAP consistency (Name, Address, Phone Number): Make sure your business details are exactly identical on your website, in GMB and in every other online directory (e.g. Yelp, Gelbe Seiten – the German Yellow Pages –, local business directories). Inconsistencies confuse AIs and hurt your ranking.
  5. Use local keywords and long-tail keywords: Research what your target audience searches for locally. That can be street names, neighborhoods, landmarks or specific local problems.
    • Example: “Emergency plumber Marienplatz Munich” or “Restaurant with vegan options Prenzlauer Berg”.
  6. Local links and mentions: Work on getting links from other local websites (local blogs, news portals, tourism associations). Mentions (citations) without a direct link are valuable too, as long as NAP consistency is in place.
  7. Schema markup for local businesses: Implement local schema markup (structured data) on your website. This helps AIs understand your business information (address, phone number, opening hours, reviews etc.) precisely and integrate it directly into answers.
  8. Content about local events and news: Write about local events, charity initiatives, local history or news that’s relevant to your audience. It shows local expertise and engagement.
  9. Optimize for voice search: Since more and more people use voice search, phrase your content conversationally, in a way that answers natural questions. Think about how someone would look for your services by voice.
    • Example: Instead of “Best pizzeria Berlin”, a voice search might be: “Where can I find a good pizzeria that delivers near me in Berlin-Kreuzberg?”

Action plan for content creators, managing directors and marketing directors

For content creators / copywriters:

  1. Content analysis & filling gaps:
    • Identify your top keywords and think about which questions those keywords raise.
    • Analyze which questions your existing content doesn’t answer fully.
    • Use tools like AnswerThePublic or the “People Also Ask” section in Google to research which questions your audience is asking.
  2. Use structured data:
    • Implement schema markup (JSON-LD) for your business information, products/services, FAQs and reviews. That makes your data machine-readable.
    • Use headings (H1, H2, H3), lists and tables to keep content easy to scan.
  3. Keep up the question-and-answer format:
    • Create FAQ sections on your landing pages.
    • Write paragraphs so they can answer a question directly.
    • Use tables of contents for longer articles.
  4. Authority through expertise:
    • Make sure your texts are written or reviewed by experts (E-E-A-T: Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, Trustworthiness). Name authors and their qualifications.
    • Link to trustworthy external sources where appropriate.
  5. Create local landing pages:
    • Create a separate, unique landing page for every relevant location or service area.
    • Enrich these pages with specific local keywords, opening hours, directions and local customer testimonials.
  6. Freshness and relevance:
    • Review and update your content regularly. AIs value outdated information less.
    • Write blog posts about local events, news or trends that affect your business.

For managing directors and marketing directors:

  1. Budget for technical SEO and content strategy:
    • Invest in tools and experts who help you implement schema markup and analyze your website structure.
    • Make sure your CMS meets the technical requirements for an AI-optimized website (e.g. fast load times, mobile friendliness).
  2. Train your people:
    • Train your marketing and content team in the basics of AI optimization and why structured data matters.
    • Encourage a data-driven approach to content creation.
  3. Make Google Business Profile a priority:
    • Don’t treat your Google Business Profile as an afterthought, but as a central marketing tool.
    • Appoint one person responsible for maintaining and optimizing it.
  4. Push reputation management:
    • Actively managing online reviews is crucial. Encourage happy customers to leave reviews and respond professionally to negative feedback.
  5. Strengthen local partnerships:
    • Build relationships with other local businesses, organizations and media to generate local links and mentions.
  6. Adopt the “answer” mindset:
    • Understand that the future of search is answering questions directly. Adapt your entire content strategy to this paradigm shift. Think in answers, not just keywords.
  7. Test and analyze:
    • Use analytics tools to track how your content performs in the new search landscape. Adjust your strategy based on what you learn.

The era of AI-driven search engines isn’t just a threat, it’s also a huge opportunity. If you prepare your content to be precise, fact-based and well structured – with a strong focus on local relevance – you can make sure your business stays visible and successful in a world where AIs deliver the answers. The time to set the course is now. Whoever acts today will reap the rewards tomorrow.
