Services

Three things I do really well. Everything else – design, film, development, media – comes from my network, and I coordinate.

1 / 3 · For whom: Founders, CEOs and teams in companies without their own IT department who want to use AI day to day – in marketing, sales, administration, customer service.

AI consulting & rollout

Everyone is talking about it, a few people try ChatGPT, nobody has a plan. Licences get bought and not used. Fear of data protection blocks things; so does the fear of falling behind.

What happens

  1. AI audit: I look at your workflows and find the five places where AI actually saves time – not the fifty that theoretically work.
  2. Workshops with the team: hands-on, with your real tasks, not with examples from the internet. After one day everyone in the room can do something they couldn’t before.
  3. Setup: which tools, which access, which rules (data protection, approvals, quality control). Prompts and templates that fit your work.
  4. Follow-through: I stay on it until it runs day to day. Monthly check: what works, what doesn’t, what’s new.

And data protection?

The most common question – and the most common reason nothing happens. The answer isn’t “no”, it’s a set of rules: which data may go into which tool, which never. Models with EU servers and data processing agreements where customer data is involved. No training on your data. Approvals before anything goes out. I set this up with you before the first team member writes a prompt – so the CEO sleeps at night and the team can still work.

What you get

  • Audit report with priorities and effort
  • Workshop day(s) with your team
  • Tool setup, guidelines, templates
  • Monthly follow-up as needed

How we measure success

  • Hours saved per week and person
  • Turnaround time for proposals, copy, reports
  • Share of the team using the tools weekly

Formats

Audit (2–3 days), workshop day, ongoing support.

2 / 3 · For whom: Companies, associations and brands that need an experienced marketing lead but don’t want or can’t afford a full-time hire.

Marketing management

The agency delivers pretty concepts and little execution. The in-house person is good but alone, with no sparring partner. Social media happens on the side. Nobody knows what any of it actually does.

What happens

  1. Strategy: where we stand, who we want to reach, what we say, on which channels – on two pages, not sixty slides.
  2. Brand: positioning, tone, visual identity. Rebranding with specialists from my network where needed.
  3. Content and social media: editorial plan, production, community. I know the platforms from the inside and what works this year.
  4. Website and digital presence: concept, build, accessibility, SEO and GEO (visibility in AI search).
  5. Steering: budget, suppliers, reporting. One point of contact for everything.

What it costs – and how we get the most out of it

I work on a day rate or a fixed monthly budget, without agency overhead: no account manager, no junior learning on your time, no 15% handling on third-party costs. The marketing budget itself is allocated by a simple principle: test small first, then measure, then shift to where the numbers are best. Every month. What doesn’t deliver gets stopped – even if it was my idea. That’s how a medium budget gets more out than a big one planned once a year.

What you get

  • Marketing strategy and annual plan
  • Ongoing operation of content, social, website
  • Monthly report in plain language

How we measure success

  • Reach and engagement per channel
  • Enquiries, bookings, applications
  • Cost per contact

Formats

Interim (full time for a few months), one fixed day a week, on call.

3 / 3 · For whom: Brands and companies that want to become visible and win customers on Instagram, TikTok, LinkedIn or Facebook – organically and with ads. And that have mostly just posted or bought reach so far.

Social media & paid ads

The posts go out, the campaign runs, the click price is fine – and the business doesn’t come. Usually it’s not the audience, it’s the content: the ad looks like an ad, the landing page doesn’t match, the offer is unclear.

What happens

  1. Social media strategy: channels, audience, topics, content calendar, tone of voice. What we build organically and where ads make the difference.
  2. Campaign planning: goal, audience, offer, budget structure, test plan. What we want to learn before we scale.
  3. Content production: video, photo, copy, landing pages – in the formats that work on each platform. With AI where it’s faster and cheaper, with real people where it has to look real.
  4. Operation: posting, community management, campaign setup, tracking, ongoing optimisation, weekly tests of new creatives.
  5. Reporting: what cost what, what brought what, what we do differently next week.

Glossy is over

A TV spot still needs perfect light and a model. On social media, in ads and on websites, the real thing wins today: the employee instead of the actor, the phone video from the set instead of the studio production, the small mistake that shows it actually happened. Users spot advertising in half a second and scroll on – authenticity holds them. That doesn’t mean sloppy, it means deliberate: produced fast, told honestly, in the language of the platform.

What you get

  • Social media strategy and content calendar
  • Campaign concept with budget plan
  • Content, creatives and landing pages
  • Ongoing management and weekly report

How we measure success

  • Reach and engagement of your own channels
  • Cost per lead, enquiry or purchase
  • Return on ad spend
  • Landing page conversion rate

Formats

Project (build-up or campaign with a start and an end) or ongoing management of your channels with a monthly budget.
