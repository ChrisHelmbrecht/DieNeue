When I scroll through Instagram these days, I can tell immediately who has ChatGPT write their texts – and who doesn't. AI language is now as recognisable as stock photos in a corporate brochure.

The structure, the tone, the emojis. All algorithm-friendly – but also interchangeable.

And yes, ChatGPT (or Midjourney, DALL·E, Firefly) **could put together your entire social media presence today**:

*"Write me an Instagram post about XYZ, create a matching image, turn it into a LinkedIn carousel and please generate three Stories for TikTok as well."*

That works. Technically. But it isn't enough.

## The new one-size-fits-all mush

You can see the result every day:

🚀 Far too many posts with the same emojis.

🎯 The same buzzwords.

💡 The same AI voice.

What's missing? Personality. A point of view. Rough edges.

That's not a criticism of the tools – it's a criticism of those who use them badly or lazily. When the intern quickly "puts something out" to tick the box. When there's no clear strategy. When authenticity is replaced by automation.

## What AI can do now

ChatGPT can analyse an uploaded image, recognise the target platform, come up with a content idea and write it right away so that it matches the current state of the literature on the algorithm.

Since ChatGPT got access to the internet, it analyses new studies, blog posts and tips from social media pros every day. The algorithm itself may be secret – but the tools know pretty well how to feed it.

That means: in theory, I could have 30 posts a week generated today, in 10 languages, on 5 platforms – and everything works.

But only on the surface.

## Welcome to the mush

If everyone does exactly that – what comes out?

A sea of interchangeable, watered-down posts with the same emojis and calls to action over and over again.

And by the third post at the latest, your follower knows too: that wasn't the human, that was the machine.\
Because authenticity is missing.

Because a point of view is missing.

Because there's no "I" – just an algorithm-friendly average.

## And what does the algorithm do then?

Exactly. It goes one layer deeper.\
When **everyone** meets the same formal criteria, the algorithm measures what only humans can influence:

- time spent
- interaction
- emotional response
- relevance

**No prompt decides that. Your audience does.**

## And this is where marketing comes in

I've been in marketing for decades.

I've helped build brands, sharpened positioning, done audience analyses, developed strategies and run advertising campaigns – from the Dow Jones corporation to the local restaurant.

And I'm telling you:

**Social media without a marketing strategy is a waste of time.**

Before a single post is created, you need to know:

- Who do I want to reach?
- What should land with the audience?
- What reaction do I expect?
- What is my brand identity – and how is it reflected in the content?
- How do I measure success? (Conversion? Engagement? Visibility? Leads?)
- What does my ROI look like?

**That's brand management. That's communication strategy. That's work.**

And that's exactly what no AI delivers – and no intern who plays on Instagram two hours a week either.

## What you really need today

Of course I use AI tools. Every day.

I have them suggest texts, cluster topics, work out image ideas, optimise headlines.

But I **feed the AI with strategy. With a point of view. With substance.**

I think along with my clients. And further.

That's what separates a **content machine operator** from a **real social media professional**.

And today, that professional is at the same time:

- content creator
- copywriter
- brand expert
- marketer
- community manager
- prompt designer
- and sometimes a psychologist

## What social media really delivers today

For companies, social media isn't just a mandatory channel.

It is:

- PR
- customer service
- recruiting
- community building
- brand communication
- brand management
- and, at best: a sales channel

And that's exactly why it isn't enough to just pump out posts. You need:

- **relevance**
- **coherence with the brand**
- **impact on the audience**
- **strategy – short, medium and long term**

I once worked with a client who owned two restaurants.

Former head of marketing at Danone, board member at Löwenbräu. He insisted on glossy photos of his food and his restaurants. Always. My phone photos from the real day-to-day operation bothered him a lot.

What he didn't understand: **social media lives on authenticity.**

No perfect lighting. The plate of food the way the guest gets it. The waitress's real smile. Reality.

Because that's exactly what creates trust – and relevance.

## You don't need someone who pleases the algorithm. You need someone with marketing and branding know-how and common sense.

If your social media manager only pumps out AI phrases with emojis – think hard about whether that serves your brand.

Get someone who understands brand management. Who recognises an audience. Who knows how communication works.

Who uses tools wisely – but never forgets the human behind them.
