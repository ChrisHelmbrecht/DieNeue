## What the BFSG means – and why it is more than a legal requirement

Anyone in Germany who sells products or services digitally can no longer avoid the topic of **accessibility** from mid-2025. That's because the **Barrierefreiheitsstärkungsgesetz (BFSG)**, the German Accessibility Strengthening Act, becomes binding – for many companies, even those who thought they weren't affected.

But instead of just groaning about new obligations, it's worth taking a different view: **accessibility isn't a nuisance. It is a huge opportunity. For visibility. For usability. For brand loyalty.**

## What is the BFSG anyway?

The BFSG is based on EU Directive 2019/882 (the European Accessibility Act) and requires companies to **provide certain products and services in an accessible way** – including many digital offerings such as:

- websites
- online shops
- apps
- e-readers
- ticket and self-service machines
- digital customer communication (e.g. email, chat, PDF downloads)

For many B2C providers this means: **from 28 June 2025, everything that interacts digitally with the customer must be usable without barriers.** Other EU countries have implemented the same directive in their own national laws, so the topic is not limited to Germany.

## Who exactly is affected?

All **private economic operators** offering standardised products or services – including:

- online retailers
- banks
- travel portals
- telecoms providers
- software makers
- digital media providers (e.g. publishers, streaming services)

**Micro-enterprises with fewer than 10 employees and annual turnover below €2 million are exempt**, but many agencies, service providers and mid-sized companies are fully in scope – especially if they run websites or web shops.

## What does accessible mean in the digital space?

Accessibility means: **digital content should be usable by as many people as possible, regardless of their impairments.**\
This includes, among other things:

- readability for screen readers
- contrast & colours for people with visual impairments
- keyboard navigation for people with motor impairments
- subtitles & alt texts for audiovisual content
- clear language & an understandable structure
- accessible PDF downloads
- labels & link descriptions
- responsive design

The requirements follow the international standard **WCAG 2.1 (Web Content Accessibility Guidelines)** – Level AA is the benchmark.

## Why this is not just a box-ticking exercise

**1. Google loves accessible sites**

Accessible websites are cleanly structured, fast and mobile-optimised – exactly what Google's algorithm likes too. More SEO points through better readability, clear structure and alternative image descriptions.

**2. More reach through inclusive design**

Around **10% of the population** live with a disability – many of them use screen readers or need visual aids. If you ignore this group, you actively give up potential customers.

**3. A better image through responsibility**

Accessibility isn't just an obligation, it's a strong signal. Companies that communicate accessibly and proactively show they stand for something – and that builds trust.

**4. Legal certainty and avoiding sanctions**

From mid-2025, a lack of accessibility can not only lead to cease-and-desist letters but also to sanctions – including fines. If you invest now, you save time and nerves later.

## What you should do as a company now

1. **Run an audit**
   How accessible is your website or app really? Tools like WAVE or axe help with a first check – but a professional accessibility audit is also worth it.
2. **Implement a WCAG-compliant structure**
   A clean heading hierarchy, descriptive links, clear navigation – this doesn't only help blind users.
3. **Add alt texts and image descriptions**
   Images without a description are a black hole for screen readers. Alt texts fix that – and help with SEO at the same time.
4. **Check PDFs and documents**
   Many companies forget: accessibility doesn't stop at the website. Attached documents also have to be readable, searchable and structured.
5. **Accessible UX is good UX**
   It's not only people with disabilities who benefit. A clear, accessible site helps everyone: older people, stressed parents, users with a poor internet connection – and yes, the Google bot too.
