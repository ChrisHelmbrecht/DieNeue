*(Note: this article is exclusively about the* ***algorithms*** *– the programs that decide whether and to whom your content is shown in the feeds. Of course, the* ***quality of the content*** *of a post and the* ***emotions*** *it triggers also play a big role. The app or platform measures, for example, how long someone looks at your post before swiping on, and how strong the* ***engagement*** *is (likes, comments, shares). These content factors are extremely important for success, but are only touched on here – there is a separate post about them.)*

## TikTok – the algorithm of the entertainment giant

With around 2 billion active users, TikTok is as big as Instagram – and it's long since stopped being just for teenagers; all age groups are there now. TikTok's "For You Page" (the start feed) is **highly personalised** and mainly shows videos from accounts the user doesn't know but that match their interests. The algorithm learns extremely quickly which content someone likes and puts together a unique video programme accordingly. For brands this means: **relevance and gripping content** are the key. TikTok's algorithm mainly considers three kinds of signals: **user interactions**, **video information** and **device/account settings**. In practice, what counts most is how viewers interact with your videos – watching, liking, sharing, following etc. – and what's in the videos (e.g. which hashtags, sounds or descriptions you use).

### TikTok – checklist for more reach (as of July 2025):

- **Post often and consistently:** TikTok's own business portal recommends publishing **1–4 videos per day** if possible. Very few manage that many – but the more regular content, the better. According to the data, even **3–5 videos per week** can bring significantly higher engagement rates than sporadic posting. What matters is a steady rhythm so the algorithm sees you as an "active" content supplier.
- **Start videos with a strong hook:** TikTok's algorithm weights **watch time** enormously – the first seconds of a video are especially critical. So try to win attention right at the start (e.g. with a surprising scene, a question or an exciting teaser). Long watch time and viewing to the end are quality signals that lead to your video being shown to more people.
- **Use trends, sounds and hashtags:** TikTok's algorithm categorises videos based on **caption text, hashtags and the sound used**. So research current trends and sounds in your niche and work them in where they fit. The right hashtags (e.g. community trends like *#BookTok* or *#FoodTok*) help the algorithm assign your content to the right interest groups. That increases the chance of showing up in your audience's feeds.
- **Serve your niche:** Don't try to address everyone at once – **deliver content that goes down well in a specific community**. TikTok consists of many subcultures and "communities" with their own humour and style. If your videos are relevant to a particular niche (e.g. #CraftsmanTips, #FitnessOver40 etc.), the algorithm will show them to those people more. Micro-virality within an engaged niche community often brings more than a random mainstream hit.
- **Interact with the community:** TikTok rewards not only viewing behaviour but also **creator activity**. Reply to comments on your videos, ask the community questions in your captions and encourage duets or stitch responses. Every interaction (commenting, sharing, following) sends positive signals. The algorithm also understands you better if you are active on the platform yourself. A lively exchange with viewers can lead to future videos being recommended more often to these engaged users and similar communities.
- **Follow the guidelines & deliver quality:** Make sure not to violate the **community guidelines** – offensive or dangerous content is made invisible. Low-quality videos (e.g. very blurry or obviously recycled with watermarks from other platforms) also perform worse. TikTok wants to keep users entertained: **high-quality, creative and entertaining clips** have the best chance of being picked up by the algorithm. Better to put more effort into a few really good videos than to post lots of half-hearted clips.

## Instagram – the AI-driven mix for feed, Stories & Reels

In 2025, Instagram relies fully on **AI-powered ranking systems** instead of rigid rules. For you as a social media manager this means: Instagram's "algorithm" constantly analyses user behaviour and adapts. Every user sees an individually sorted mix of posts from people they follow (connected reach) and suggested content from accounts they don't know (unconnected reach). In the feed, posts appear near the top when Instagram predicts the user will find them interesting – measured, for example, by the likelihood that they will **stay on it for more than 10 seconds or write a comment**. In 2025, Instagram head Adam Mosseri named **time spent**, **likes** and **shares** (how often your post was sent on directly) as the most important factors for a post's success. The same applies to Stories (replies, likes, taps) and Reels (views, comments, shares).

### Instagram – checklist for the algorithm (as of July 2025):

- **3–5 feed posts per week:** A regular posting rhythm is crucial. Our recommendation: around **three to five feed posts per week**, rather than everything on one day. That way you appear consistently in your followers' feeds. Instagram rewards regular activity – according to Mosseri, accounts usually grow faster **the more (good-quality) content they post**, because more people can then discover and share a post. You should also **be present in Stories every day** (at least one Story, ideally around two a day) to keep the bond with your existing followers. Stories have hardly any influence on reaching new people, but they keep your current audience engaged, which indirectly helps the feed algorithm.
- **Use the different formats – above all Reels:** Instagram offers feed photos, carousel posts, videos, Reels and Stories. **Use this mix**, because different formats increase the chances of interaction. **Reels** are especially important: these short videos are pushed hard and also reach non-followers via the Explore page and the Reels tab. So plan one or two Reels every week. Pay attention to the criteria Instagram has defined for recommendable Reels: **no watermark** (i.e. not copied 1:1 from TikTok), **with audio** (use music or original sound), **ideally under 3 minutes long**, and **original content** (no TV recordings etc.). If you stick to these rules, the chances increase that your Reels appear in the recommendations. Carousels (multi-image posts) are also worth it: users swipe longer, which increases **watch time / time spent** – a positive ranking factor in the feed.
- **Use Stories wisely:** Stories are ranked algorithmically by how closely a follower is connected to you (interactions, DMs etc.) and whether they generally like watching your Stories. Keep your **Story frequency high (daily)**, but without spamming – a few Story slides a day are enough. Use interactive stickers (polls, quizzes, questions), because **the more often someone taps on or replies to your Story, the more often and further forward** your Story will be shown to them in future. Stories are perfect for sharing daily insights, polls or quick tips without overloading the feed. They strengthen brand loyalty and give the algorithm more signals about who is interested in you.
- **Actively encourage engagement:** **Interaction is everything** on Instagram. Posts that get lots of likes, comments, saves or shares signal high value to the algorithm and are shown in more feeds. So build in calls to action: ask questions in the caption ("What do you think? Tell us in the comments"), invite people to tag friends ("Tag someone who needs to know this"), or encourage sharing ("Share this tip in your Story"). In 2025, **shares** and **saves** in particular count as strong indicators of quality – Meta has confirmed that "sends" (posts forwarded to others) are among the top signals. Watch your insights: if a particular post was viewed for an unusually long time or shared a lot, that's exactly the kind of content you should build on.
- **Pick up on trends, but stay authentic:** On Instagram too, you can benefit from current trends (e.g. viral challenges, memes, audio trends in Reels). If it fits your brand, join in – the algorithm takes into account how **topically relevant** a post is to the zeitgeist. Use 3–5 relevant **hashtags** per post to increase the chance of appearing in searches or on Explore pages (hashtags should reflect the topic, not just a generic #fyp). Important: stay **authentic** throughout. Avoid "engagement bait" like *"Write YES in the comments"* – Instagram increasingly recognises such clumsy tricks and penalises them. Instead, ask honest questions or call for user-generated content (e.g. "Post a photo with our product and tag us") to create real conversation.
- **Keep your account healthy:** Make sure your profile doesn't risk a shadowban or similar. **Violations of the guidelines** (e.g. offensive content, false promises) lead to your posts being downgraded by the algorithm or not shown at all. Check your **account status** in the settings (as Instagram recommends) to make sure everything is in order. External factors such as a longer period of inactivity can also have an effect – Instagram rewards current, regular posters. Bottom line: stay active, but high-quality and within the rules, and the AI will deliver your posts to your followers and beyond.

## Facebook – meaningful interactions are the key

With over 3 billion users, Facebook still has the largest reach. However, organic visibility for business pages is hardest here: the average engagement rate for page posts is below 1%. Why? Facebook's algorithm (which is also driven by **AI and "personalised ranking"**) prefers to show users content involving **friends, family or closely connected groups** – i.e. posts from people and groups they interact with frequently. Content from pages (brands) therefore has to offer real relevance or discussion to appear in the news feed. In 2025, Facebook increasingly emphasises **"authentic engagement"**: posts that **spark conversations** are prioritised, while passive posts without reactions lose reach. Facebook now also mixes more "recommended content" into the feed, but only when the algorithm thinks it is valuable for the user. For pages this means: without interaction, hardly any visibility. Here's what you can do:

### Facebook – checklist for the algorithm (as of July 2025):

- **1–2 posts per day, no more:** More than two unpromoted posts a day can overwhelm fans, and the second post competes with the first for attention. **One or two high-quality posts per day** are ideal. That keeps you present in your followers' feeds without spamming them. Important: if you only have a little truly good content, even **2–3 excellent posts per week** are better than mediocre content every day. Quality over quantity – this applies especially on Facebook.
- **Create posts with conversation potential:** Facebook's algorithm loves **posts that trigger comments and discussions**. In 2025, more than ever: **"meaningful interactions"** are the decisive factor. So ask your community questions ("What do you think about XYZ?"), ask for users' tips or experiences, or post opinion topics your followers want to weigh in on. Controversial theses (presented in a factual, appropriate way) can also generate lots of comments – but moderate politely. **Without engagement, reach drops drastically**: if a post gets no likes or comments within the first hours, the algorithm will hardly show it to anyone any more. The goal is therefore to provoke reactions as soon as you publish. Schedule your posts for times when your audience is online so that interactions happen quickly.
- **Use interactive and visual formats:** Text updates alone have a hard time. **Images, videos and especially live videos** perform better – anything that holds users' attention longer. According to experts, in 2025 we're seeing very good results with **interactive content** like **polls, quiz questions or live broadcasts**, because they involve users directly. Consider, for example, starting a simple Facebook poll or a short quiz in the comments. Posts like these invite people to take part and extend the time someone spends with your post – which in turn raises the "relevance score" Facebook internally gives every post. **Videos** are still strong as well: Facebook counts every view, however short, as engagement, and longer views have a positive effect. Short clips (e.g. 1–2 minutes) or **Facebook Reels** (15–30 seconds) can reach a large audience, because Facebook increasingly suggests short videos from pages to people who don't follow them. A mix is ideal: sometimes a question as a text post, sometimes a funny team photo, sometimes a tutorial video, etc., to serve different audience preferences. The algorithm mixes different post types anyway to keep the feed varied.
- **Mind timing & diversity:** Facebook's feed isn't strictly chronological, but **recency** plays a role – newer posts have an advantage, especially on trending topics. So feel free to post about relevant current events that affect your audience (e.g. industry topics, seasonal events, news). A post on a trending topic that gets lots of reactions is recommended more often to other users with similar interests. On the other hand, avoid posting several very similar posts right after each other (say, three link posts in a row) – the algorithm deliberately mixes the feed to show variety. So vary the content: e.g. a video in the morning, an image with a short story in the afternoon, a link to a blog article the next day, etc. That keeps you in the feed "mix". And if you plan several posts a day, leave a few hours between them so the first post has enough time to collect engagement before the next one comes.
- **Actively involve fans and the community:** Unlike on TikTok or Instagram, on Facebook **interaction with existing fans** is often what drives any reach at all. **Reply to comments** under your posts – ideally quickly, while the post is still "hot". Every additional comment level (thread) counts as more engagement and can increase time spent. Also encourage your followers to share content (e.g. "Share the video if you liked it"), because **shared content reaches non-followers**, and that's often the only way to reach new people organically. Facebook also ranks content shared by friends higher, as it counts as a kind of trust signal. If it suits your brand, you can also use **Facebook Groups** strategically: share particularly helpful posts in groups on related topics, or even start your own community group. Posts that get lots of approval in **active groups** are also rated positively by the algorithm, and so indirectly appear in more members' feeds. Important: this has to be authentic – blunt self-promotion in groups is more likely to be removed than liked.
- **Facebook Stories & Reels as a complement:** Cracking the classic news feed is hard – so it's fine to rely on **additional channels**. Facebook Stories (similar to Instagram Stories) are good for daily insights or announcements that appear right at the top for your fans. The reach of Stories is limited to your followers, but they keep your brand present. **Facebook Reels**, on the other hand, are also shown to non-fans. So if you have short videos, publish them both on Instagram and as a Reel on Facebook. In 2025, Facebook's algorithm is strongly video-focused and tries to win over a younger audience with Reels – you can benefit from that by providing suitable snackable content. In short: use every option (feed, Stories, Reels, groups) to increase the likelihood that your content finds its way into the feeds.

## X (Twitter) – an algorithm built around the here and now

On X (formerly Twitter), everything revolves around **real time** and conversation. X's algorithm essentially consists of two feeds: **"For you"** (sorted algorithmically, including tweets from accounts you don't follow) and **"Following"** (chronological, from the accounts you follow). Most users see the "For you" stream first, which, like TikTok, is put together individually. To do this, the X algorithm analyses which tweets and topics are hot right now, what similar users are interested in and who you interact with. It then tries to predict which tweets you are likely to like, reply to or retweet. Overall: **current, relevant and lively content** performs best. Unlike Instagram and the rest, though, tweets have a very short lifespan – often only a few minutes before the next wave of new tweets arrives. That's why **posting frequency** matters more here and the algorithm relies heavily on **recency** (how current a tweet is). After Elon Musk's takeover, parts of the algorithm were also made public, showing that **engagement rates, account verification and interaction networks** have a big influence. Here are concrete tips:

### X (Twitter) – checklist for the algorithm (as of July 2025):

- **Tweet several times a day:** On X, content goes stale extremely quickly. To stay visible, you should post **at least 2–3 tweets a day**. Many successful accounts tweet considerably more often. But it's important to **spread tweets over the day** rather than posting them all at once. If you have 5 ideas, schedule them in the morning, at midday, in the afternoon and in the evening, for example. The algorithm filters content out when too many consecutive tweets come from the same person – nobody should have to see 10 posts in a row from the same account. You also reach different users at different times of day. Use tools or X's scheduling function to stagger tweets. Consistency pays off: an active account is more likely to be suggested to others and stays present for your followers.
- **Include visual content:** Tweets with **images, GIFs, videos or polls** demonstrably achieve higher engagement rates and are favoured by the algorithm. Purely text-based tweets can go viral too, but the statistics speak for visual hooks. Even a single image or a short animation can increase attention and lead to more likes and retweets. If it fits the content, build in media – e.g. infographics, product photos, short clips or the occasional meme GIF to lighten things up. **Polls** are strong too: they generate interactions with little effort on the user's side and signal interest to the algorithm. The X system rates tweets with a higher engagement score better and then shows them to even more people.
- **Optimise for engagement:** As on every platform, **engagement is the booster**. **Replies** and **retweets** in particular carry your tweet into ever wider circles. The algorithm looks closely at the number of these interactions. Ask yourself with every tweet: would I react to this as a reader? Some proven tactics: **ask questions** (the community likes to answer when it can share expertise), **share controversial opinions** (leads to replies agreeing or disagreeing, but be careful with polarisation), **use calls to action** ("Retweet if you agree" – but sparingly), **start short threads** (multi-part tweets often spark discussion). **Trending hashtags** in the text can also help increase visibility – but only if your tweet really contributes something to the trend. A tweet that, for example, comments on a current news story and quickly gets lots of likes and replies is recognised by the system as "valuable" and appears for even more people in the "For you" feed.
- **Be quick and conversational:** X is a conversation platform. **Timely reactions** play a big role. When users reply to your tweet, try to respond as quickly as possible – whether with a follow-up question, a witty comeback or a thank-you. This lively **real-time interaction** is encouraged by the platform. On the one hand, it keeps your tweets current in the conversation (recency); on the other, it shows the algorithm that your account is active. In addition, the followers of the person replying may also see your tweet, which leads to new impressions. Another advantage: if your tweet already has some replies, the next user feels more encouraged to add their opinion too (social proof). So keep the dialogue going, especially in the first hour after publishing – that's often when it's decided whether a tweet takes off or sinks.
- **Use trends and hashtags deliberately:** **Trending topics** are gold on X. Check the trending hashtag list (local and global) every day to see if anything fits your brand or industry. If so, join the conversation with a smart tweet. Tweets about current events or popular hashtags are preferentially shown by the algorithm to users who follow that trend or have generally shown interest in it. But be careful: just jumping on a trend for its own sake ("Happy #NationalPizzaDay!" with no connection) looks opportunistic and achieves little. It's better to offer a unique angle or added value on the trending topic so your tweet stands out. An example: a software company could pick up an ongoing AI trend and post a short expert assessment under the trending hashtag – relevant and visible. Use **hashtags** sparingly (one to three per tweet is enough), but deliberately. Industry or event hashtags increase your discoverability among people who are specifically searching for them.
- **Use account reputation and features:** X's algorithm also takes **account quality** and settings into account. Verified accounts or profiles with high credibility have advantages. It may be worth considering **paid verification** if it fits your strategy – as of 2025, verified content tends to be shown preferentially in the "For you" feed. Keep a healthy follower/following ratio (lots of followers while blindly following tens of thousands yourself looks dubious). And stick to the rules: accounts that have spread spam or violated guidelines in the past are throttled. Technically it helps to use **new X features** (e.g. longer posts, polls, audio Spaces), as such posts are often given a bit of an algorithmic push to encourage adoption. Bottom line: **be a good X user** – deliver relevant content, interact with others, stay polite and original. Then the algorithm won't put obstacles in your way, but will at best carry your tweets into the feeds of people who don't even know you yet.

## LinkedIn – relevance through expertise and conversation

LinkedIn differs from the platforms above because it is a *professional* network. Users there are more likely to look for professional value, industry news and networking opportunities. The **LinkedIn algorithm in 2025** aims to show every user relevant, interesting posts for their professional interests. Especially important: **who is posting and about what**. LinkedIn tries to supply you with content from people in your network or your field. The ranking process runs in three stages: first a **quality check** (spam filter), then an **engagement test** (a small share of your followers sees the post – based on their reactions, it is decided whether more people see it), and finally **network distribution** (if it goes down well, the post may also appear for 2nd- and 3rd-degree connections).

For us this means: *no initial engagement, no large audience.* LinkedIn says it looks especially closely at the **first hour ("golden hour") after posting**. Posts are also sorted into categories: **spam, low quality or high quality**. Anything that looks like spam (e.g. randomly tagging 20 people, 20 hashtags, a daily flood of posts) is penalised. **Valuable content with real interaction**, by contrast, is rewarded. Interestingly, not every like is worth the same – **high-quality comments from professionals count more** than superficial engagement. LinkedIn wants *professional conversations* to happen. Here's a roadmap for achieving that:

### LinkedIn – checklist for the algorithm (as of July 2025):

- **Quality over quantity – about 1 post a day:** LinkedIn itself says companies that post **weekly** achieve twice as much engagement. According to its materials, the optimum is even **at least once a day**, weekends included. But careful: unlike on X, you **shouldn't post too often in one day**. Too much frequency can be classified as spam by the algorithm – a gap of **at least ~12 hours between two posts** is advisable. One post a day (or every other day) is a good guideline for active pages, as long as you have enough content ideas. What matters is that **every post delivers value** (see below). If you have a small team, 2–3 excellent posts a week are absolutely fine – in the long run, LinkedIn rewards consistency more than sheer volume. Plan your posting days and times so that you are online in the first hour after publishing to respond to any comments.
- **Stick to your subject (show expertise):** LinkedIn rewards profiles and pages that build up a **topical focus**. If you consistently post about a particular subject area (relevant to your business), the algorithm will over time recognise you as an **authority** in that field. Such posts then tend to be **distributed more widely**, beyond your direct followers too. So think about which core messages or areas of knowledge you want to cover. For example, regularly share **industry trends, how-tos, lessons learned** or **insider insights** from your field. This not only helps the algorithm categorise you but also attracts the right readers (who are more likely to comment and like because it's their topic). Of course you can share off-topic, human insights now and then, but the majority of your posts should **strengthen your brand as an expert**. A side effect: your profile gains visibility, because LinkedIn prefers to suggest posts from known experts to new people.
- **Real discussion instead of clickbait:** As with Facebook: **meaningful engagement** counts. On LinkedIn this means that a post with **10 detailed comments from fellow professionals** is far more valuable than one with 50 "Nice post!" comments or 100 likes. So encourage **qualified comments**. Ask, for example: "How do you handle XYZ in your company?", "What's your biggest challenge with …?" or share a controversial industry opinion that makes people think and respond. But be sure to avoid **engagement baiting**. The algorithm now recognises phrases like *"Comment YES if…"* or *"Agree? Then like!"* and classifies such posts as low quality. Better: ask open questions that can't be answered with just yes/no, or ask for personal experiences. One more tip: **be active on LinkedIn yourself**, not just on your own profile. If you often comment thoughtfully on other people's posts, you increase your visibility in the network and build relationships – those people are then also more likely to see your posts and comment back. Community care is a real algorithm lever on LinkedIn.
- **Use the "golden hour":** LinkedIn first tests your post with a fraction of your followers. **If the post manages to collect a good amount of interaction in the first hour** (comments, reactions, shares), it is shown to more people – even people outside your network (e.g. *"Person X, whom you know, commented on this post…"*). This mechanism was strengthened further in 2025. So try to boost the critical early phase: perhaps share the post internally with colleagues or friendly industry contacts and ask them to add their thoughts underneath if the content is relevant to them. Organically, of course – no dull LinkedIn pods posting "Great!" underneath (the algorithm notices). But a genuine first comment can get the ball rolling. Replies to comments also count as engagement – so be sure to be active in this first hour and answer every reaction as quickly as possible. That way your post signals: something is happening here, and LinkedIn rewards that with more reach.
- **Post type: native and helpful content is preferred:** LinkedIn wants users to stay on the platform. That's why **posts with external links** (that lead away from LinkedIn) get significantly less distribution. If, for example, you want to share a blog article, consider an alternative to a bare link: summarise the key points as a short text post and mention that the full article is linked in the comments (then post the link as a comment). It's even better to use **LinkedIn's own formats**: write a **newsletter article** on your profile now and then, post a short **video directly on LinkedIn** instead of a YouTube link, or use the **document feature** (PDFs/slide decks people can flip through). Such "document posts" – also called carousels – often perform extremely well because they keep users in the post longer and deliver a lot of value. According to LinkedIn, **videos** get 5× more engagement on average and live videos even 24× more than normal posts. Live isn't for everyone, of course, but how about short how-to videos or interviews? **Images/graphics** are worth it too: visual posts get about twice as many comments as pure text posts. Overall: **offer real value** (e.g. tips, checklists, industry insights), and people are more likely to save or share your post – which in turn signals to the algorithm that it should show this content to more users.
- **Use hashtags & formatting deliberately:** LinkedIn allows hashtags, but restrained use is advisable. **3–5 relevant hashtags** per post are ideal. Choose specific ones (#OnlineMarketingTips) rather than ultra-generic ones (#business) so you show up in suitable niche feeds. Don't overdo it – a jumble of hashtags looks like spam. Likewise, only tag people if they really relate to the post – the algorithm treats random tagging as an attempt at spam. As for formatting: long blocks of text are best avoided. Break up your post with paragraphs, emojis as bullet points or a question at the start – that increases **dwell time**, because people are more likely to keep reading. And yes, **dwell time** (how long someone stays on your post) is now a big factor: the longer your post is read, the more LinkedIn rates it as interesting. That's why **lists, step-by-step guides or storytelling posts** work well, for example – they draw the reader in.

Finally: **stay helpful and authentic.** LinkedIn users don't like a blunt sales pitch as a post. Focus instead on sharing knowledge and sparking discussions – that's how you organically gain a reach no ad can buy. The algorithm will reward you with visibility **when others find your content valuable**. And you achieve that by focusing on real networking and quality content.

**Conclusion:** Every platform has its quirks – TikTok's algorithm loves gripping short videos and a high posting frequency, Instagram rewards creative use of all features and interaction, Facebook relies on meaningful conversations, X on timeliness and lively participation, and LinkedIn on professional value and expert discussion. Despite all the differences, there is a common denominator: **in the end, content wins that genuinely gives the audience something and triggers a reaction.** If you take the tips above to heart and "press the right buttons" on each algorithm, you increase the chance that your posts are shown prominently in the feed – both to your followers and to new people. But always remember: the algorithm only opens the door. **You have to walk through it with good content that excites people.** With that in mind: good luck "feeding" the algorithms – and above all, creating posts that are worth spreading!
