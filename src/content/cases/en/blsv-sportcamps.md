## Situation

The Bavarian State Sports Association runs sports camps at Spitzingsee, in Regen in the Bavarian Forest and in northern Bavaria: accommodation for clubs, school classes, holiday camps and group trips. Good houses, good locations, full in peak season – and room to grow outside it.

Online, the camps practically didn’t exist in 2022. An unmaintained Facebook page, no other channels, websites that were hard to use on a phone. Anyone googling “sports camp Bavarian Forest” ended up elsewhere. And from June 2025 the German accessibility act came on top.

## Solution

Not everything at once, but in the order that pays off fastest:

1. **Build social media** – Instagram, Facebook, YouTube, TikTok, Google Business Profile. Spitzingsee first as a pilot, then the other camps. Content produced on site: camp, landscape, sport, kitchen, people.
2. **New websites** – for Spitzingsee, Regen, Nordbayern and the Sportschule Oberhaching. Mobile first, enquiry forms where visitors need them, accessible under the new law.
3. **Reporting in plain language** – quarterly reports for the board and camp managers: how many people come, from where, what they do, how many enquire. Every number with one sentence on what it means.

## Execution

I’m the marketing coordinator for the camps on a freelance basis, one fixed day a week plus production on site. Social media and websites I do myself; film and photo partly with people from my network. I use AI for research, copy variants, image editing and preparing analytics data for the reports.

## Result

The camps are now present on Instagram, Facebook, YouTube, TikTok and Google Business, with six-figure organic reach per quarter – without paid advertising. Around 70% of website visits come via unpaid Google search: anyone searching for “sports camp Bavarian Forest” today finds the camp.

The number that counts for the association: enquiries via the website. They multiplied within one quarter once the new website was live and the forms sat where visitors need them. The reports show the board every quarter where enquiries come from and what has changed.

## What I learned

For an association, reach doesn’t count – the enquiry does. So in every report the number of enquiries sits at the top, and reach below it, as an explanation of where the enquiries come from. And: the bridge from social media to the website is the weakest link. Less than one percent of website visits come from social. That’s what we’re working on now.
